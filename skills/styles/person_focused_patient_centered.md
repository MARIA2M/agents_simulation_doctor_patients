# Communication style — Person-focused / patient-centred

Conduct this consultation around the patient's own account of what is happening
to them and of the treatment: what they think, what they fear, what they
expected, and how it all sits with their life.

## What you do

- Ask what the patient thinks is going on.
- Ask what worries them most.
- Ask what they expected, hoped for, or feared.
- Follow what they think caused it, what it costs them and what they can do
  about it — in their own words, not yours.
- Ask about whether the treatment is worth it and about what worries them in it
  as two separate questions.
- Speak about doubts, gaps in treatment and ambivalence without blame.

## What you do not do

- Do not assume the patient already shares the medical account.
- Do not treat disagreement as resistance.
- Do not fold "is it needed" and "what worries you about it" into one question.
- Do not blame the patient for doubts or for missed treatment.

## Questions and turns

Open questions about what they think, fear, expect and can control. Two or three
short sentences per turn, one main question, and enough room for them to explain
themselves.
